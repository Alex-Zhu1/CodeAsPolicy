# Operating knowledge for RoboDojo: how the tools behave (no task recipes)

## Robot conventions
- Two ARX-X5 arms, `"left"` and `"right"`, stand side by side at the near edge
  of a table; the head camera looks down the table from the robot's side.
  World frame: x across the table (the LEFT arm is at negative x and on the
  LEFT of the head image), y away from the robot towards the far edge, z up;
  metres and degrees.  The arms' bases are at x = -0.30 and +0.30, y = -0.45.
- Motion positions refer to the finger-pad centre TCP (`tcp_pos`), not the
  wrist/link position (`eef_pos`). `move_to` uses custom gripper angles in
  degrees, not XYZ Euler angles; `move_both` takes world TCP xyz or
  `robo.pose(xyz, quat)` with a unit quaternion [w,x,y,z]. Position-only
  targets preserve orientation.
- `move_to` gripper commands are CLOSE (1), OPEN (-1), KEEP (0);
  `move_both` uses `close/open/keep`. KEEP retains the last command, including
  squeeze. Opening measurements instead read 1.0 open, 0.0 closed on nothing.
  A command supplied with motion acts from its FIRST native action, not on
  arrival; use `set_gripper` at the endpoint when that timing is needed.
- `holding` indicates obstructed closure, not a verified stable grasp;
  `blocked` indicates TCP tracking lag, not confirmed contact. Inspect the
  actual result and calculate subsequent offsets from the measured TCP.
- The table top is at z = 0.765.  Read object heights from the scene
  (`locate`), never assume them.
- The task instruction (`robo.task`) is generated by the benchmark for this
  layout and is authoritative: read it before planning and obey it verbatim.
  PyRUA assembles the task card from that instruction, recorded initial state
  and images, and executor capabilities. Astra does not generate the card.
  Source labels distinguish native data, derived values and configured fallbacks;
  unknown initial measurements must not be used as target coordinates.

## The grasp point and the gripper's orientation
- `move_to` drives an arm's GRASP POINT: the middle of the finger pads, 0.145 m
  out from the end-effector link along the pointing direction.
  `state().left.tcp_pos` reports it; `eef_pos` is the link behind it.  The
  pads span about 1.2 cm either side of the grasp point and the fingertips
  reach 1.3 cm beyond it, so pointing straight down the grasp point cannot go
  lower than about 1.5 cm above the surface under it.
- The orientation is three angles (`state().left.pitch`, `.yaw`, `.roll`):
  `pitch` 0 points level and 90 straight down; `yaw` is the heading of the
  pointing direction on the table, 0 away from the robot, +90 towards the
  robot's left (-x); `roll` turns the fingers about the pointing direction.
  The starting pose is level, pointing away, `0, 0, 0`.
- Pointing straight down (`pitch=90`), `yaw` alone sets how the fingers lie:
  they close along the line at `yaw` degrees from +x (counter-clockwise seen
  from above), and `roll` reads 0. When an orientation is explicitly given,
  an EMPTY open gripper's fingers are interchangeable: `yaw` and `yaw + 180`
  are the same grasp and the move may arrive at either (`note` says so).
  A position-only move or a gripper holding something does not use this
  half-turn substitution. Check pose errors and any near-miss `note`.
- An angle you leave out of `move_to` keeps its current value: a plain
  position target keeps its full orientation. For a vertical grasp, yaw and
  roll readings can change together without a physical turn; compare poses.

## Motion intent and executor responsibilities
- Use `execute_motion(intent_id, left=..., right=..., interaction=...,
  directions=..., holding=..., completion=..., max_steps=..., reserve_steps=...)`
  for routine single/dual-arm motion. Astra chooses the goal and constraints;
  the executor chooses waypoint spacing, speed and bounded arrival correction.
  The task card preserves the native instruction and initial facts. `plan()`
  records your current goal; an executable intent is a separate fixed contract.
- Targets are absolute world TCP xyz (preserve initial orientation), a Pose, or
  `{"xyz": xyz, "angles": [pitch,yaw,roll]}`. Omitted arms hold their poses.
  `interaction="free"` asserts a currently observed clear route; `"approach"`
  means interaction only near the endpoint; `"contact"` means interaction
  throughout. Choose this physical meaning, not velocity/acceleration values.
  Each intent follows a Cartesian line. Choose a new staging goal when a route
  or grasp must change; the executor never invents a detour or object recipe.
- `directions={"right":[0,0,-1]}` permits positive motion along that world axis;
  both the target and measured path must remain in its tolerance corridor.
  Omit the constraint for an unrestricted line. Directions do not authorize
  lateral searches, back-and-forth insertion or a changed grasp.
- `holding={"right":"empty"}` asserts an observed empty hand; `"retain"`
  requires the existing closed grip to remain obstructed at every native action;
  `"keep"` preserves the command without asserting object support. No intent
  opens, closes or regrips. Use set_gripper/try_grasp for those operations and
  observe the intended object before declaring retention. A retained opening
  cannot identify an object or detect all slips. In a push with a closed empty
  gripper, declare empty plus contact; no retained-object requirement applies.
- `completion` can set position_m/orientation_deg and a short `verify` condition.
  Robot pose is checked automatically. `verify` returns as needs_observation;
  inspect the actual scene, or use the existing visual watch contract. TCP
  arrival alone never establishes insertion, intended-object grasp or release.
- `max_steps` covers the entire intent, including corrections and resumptions;
  `reserve_steps` protects episode actions for later work. Preview costly
  intents with preview=True. Budget refusal does not allow weakening a motion
  constraint. Change the allocation or strategy from the returned evidence.
- Normal checkpoints and progressing corrections stay inside the executor.
  After a known interruption, reconcile receipts and use resume_motion(id).
  It continues the remaining route from measured state with the same budget;
  it does not replay earlier travel or gripper actions. Completed IDs return
  their saved endpoint without movement. Unknown dispatch, external movement
  or a lost host session require review. Read stop_reason when needs_model is
  true. Do not wrap execute_motion/resume_motion in once(); their intent ID is
  already the execution identity. Keep once() for legacy motion and gripper calls.
- Start with a clear staging intent, then a separately observed approach/contact
  intent where geometry demands it. Avoid unnecessary segmentation inside a
  known-clear route. Low-level move_to/move_both profiles remain available for
  explicit motion requirements; routine intent execution does not need them.

## Bounded grasp attempts
- For a grasp target measured from current images, an optional bounded helper is
  `try_grasp(xyz, arm=..., pitch=..., yaw=..., roll=0, approach=[0,0,0.05],
  lift=[0,0,0.025], max_steps=64)`. Preview first to check the complete
  approach and probe plus the conservative step bound. Offsets are world
  vectors from the closure TCP, not object dimensions. Choose actual offsets
  for clearance; the helper does not sense object/finger/inter-arm collisions.
- The helper uses fast native travel and small native targets only near the
  grasp and during the probe. It stops on tracking loss, empty closure or
  closure lost during lifting; no blind position search or automatic regrasp.
  `retained` is a proprioceptive candidate, NOT object identity or a verified
  stable grasp. Show head/wrist images and check that the intended object
  followed before carrying or releasing a donor. Use a broad solid patch and
  keep finger clearance from nearby material and the other gripper.
- Put the attempt in `once()`. A partial/interrupted attempt is not permission
  to repeat it: reconcile and inspect the measured endpoint and closure, then
  continue only the remaining operation. The primitive refuses to open a
  gripper that may already hold an object. Do not retry the same geometry.
- Before closing, preview the later carry, orientation change and placement
  from reachable staging poses. A successful grasp does not prove those paths
  exist. Limit grasp retries and reserve steps for the rest of the task.

## Motion
- Single-arm moves, coordinated moves and bounded grasps share measured-progress
  feedback. At most two corrective target holds per motion are allowed while
  every missed dimension improves and budget remains. A stall returns the actual
  endpoint. Moving timed splines stop on tracking failure; corrective holds are
  allowed at their final resting target. `tracking` reports the cause and count;
  tracking error alone does not establish contact or object support.
- `move_to(xyz, arm, gripper, pitch, yaw, roll, path)` solves the arm's
  inverse kinematics for the full pose in the library (not in the simulator)
  and commands joint angles.  With `path="auto"` the grasp point follows a
  straight line, with nominal waypoints at most 2 cm / 8 degrees apart;
  joint-step limits may subdivide them. Where the line
  cannot be followed the joints swing straight to the solution (up to 0.15 rad
  per joint per action); orientation may change en route even when the target
  preserves it. Use such swings away from objects only. `path="line"` is the line
  or nothing: use it for every approach, descent and lift near objects.
  Waypoint spacing bounds geometry. Timed profiles, when supported,
  add joint derivative limits as described below.
- Use one move for a clear free-space segment. On a supported backend, use
  `move_both(via=...)` for known clear routes, including changes of direction
  and routes moving only one arm. Split calls where a new observation, contact
  decision or release is needed. Separate calls start/end at rest in the planned
  timed trajectory, so unnecessary splits add pauses. Keep orientation during
  contact approach, initial lift and retreat; make large turns after clearance.
- `reached` means within 0.01 m of the requested position and 5 degrees of the
  selected orientation, without a tracking interruption. Explicit empty-
  gripper orientations may use the equivalent half-turn described above.
  Use the actual returned pose or fresh state for the next offset, especially
  after a partial move; a requested target is not a measured endpoint.
- Nothing moves when the pose is out of reach or every way there puts the arm
  into the table: `ik_error` says which, `steps_used` is 0, and for a pose
  out of reach it names nearby poses that ARE reachable ("reachable instead:
  pitch=45 at this position, or z=0.860 with this orientation").  Take one of
  those, or the other arm; do not repeat the pose. If a nearby solution is
  within 2 cm and 8 degrees, the planner may select that pose instead;
  `note` describes the difference. Check actual arrival.
- `truncated` means no further motion: either budget exhaustion or environment
  termination. `episode_ended` is distinct from `episode_native_step_budget`;
  neither is a task success verdict, which is only read after policy completion.
- `Move.status` is `completed`, `rejected` (zero native actions), or `partial`.
  `planned_path` names selected geometry even when timing rejects it; `path`
  names executed geometry and is None when `steps_used == 0`. Neither path field
  proves completion. Inspect `reached`, `stop_reason` and the actual endpoint.
  `ik_error` is only geometric preflight detail. A timing refusal instead reports
  `unsupported_timed_trajectory`, `timed_path_exceeds_native_step_budget` or
  `trajectory_validation_failed` in `stop_reason`, with detail in `note`.
  Budget refusal does not mean the pose is unreachable. Do not repeat unchanged
  requests or loosen limits without accounting for the remaining task and budget.
- Reach depends on position and orientation. The following are reference
  samples from kinematics and joint limits, pointing away from the robot,
  not reach guarantees for other orientations. Straight down (`pitch=90`)
  the grasp point must stay LOW: at z = 0.80 an arm reaches out to y = 0.0
  (0.05 straight in front of it), at z = 0.90 to y = -0.05, at z = 1.0
  nowhere.  At `pitch=60` it reaches to y = 0.15 at z 0.80 and 0.05 at z 1.0;
  at 45 to 0.2 / 0.1; level (`pitch=0`) to about 0.3 at any of these heights.
  Across, pointing down near the table, the right arm reaches from x = -0.15
  to 0.75 at y = -0.3, from -0.1 to 0.7 at y = -0.15 and from 0.1 to 0.5 at
  y = 0.0; the left arm is the mirror image.
- Choose a top grasp when its approach, contact and lift poses are reachable.
  Use enough clearance above `top_z` for the open fingers and clear travel;
  6-8 cm is a starting estimate for low objects, not a required height.
  Take tall objects (bottles, cups) from the side, level or
  slightly tilted (`pitch` 0-30, `yaw` towards the object), at about half
  their height.  To carry something over a rim higher than about 0.9 m (a
  box, a holder) hold it at `pitch` 30-60 or level: pointing straight down
  cannot get that high.  Beyond that reach use the other arm, or push or hand
  the object closer first.
- Carrying past things: lift first (straight up, `path="line"`), travel
  above everything in the way, come down over the destination.  A container
  or fixture an arm bumps moves, and most tasks check where it ended up.
- For a push or slide, start with a 2-3 cm contact stroke and inspect the
  resulting translation/rotation. Once contact is stable and the remaining
  route is clear, a 4-6 cm stroke can avoid unnecessary stop/start cycles; shorten
  it near the target. Use `path="line"` with unchanged orientation and inspect
  after each stroke. Unintended rotation or a missed endpoint calls for a new
  contact point, not repeated pushes from the same geometry.
- After release or a push, retreat from the actual `state().arm(arm).tcp_pos`
  with orientation unchanged: a short 3-5 cm `path="line"` move back along the
  clear approach, upward for a top approach. Once clear of the object, lift only
  as far as the next route needs, then travel or turn. Keep this retreat clear
  of already placed objects; avoid a lateral sweep or wrist turn at contact.
- `blocked` flags a measured TCP more than 5 mm from the planned endpoint
  after execution with budget remaining. It is a tracking-lag heuristic,
  not a contact sensor or identification of an obstacle. Inspect before retrying.
- During a single-arm move the other arm holds its joints. Choose sequential
  calls or `move_both` according to the required cooperation; plan clearance for
  both arms, including the one that is holding still.
- To twist something held (a key, a cap, a knob), `move_to` the same point
  with another `roll` (straight down: another `yaw`); the last joint turns
  and the grasp point stays.  The wrist joint stops at about +-178 degrees.
- `home(arm)` returns one arm, or `"both"`, to the joint angles it started
  with and retains each gripper's last command. Near a recorded release it
  first withdraws 6 cm behind the release plane along the negative finger-pointing
  direction, preserving orientation. A partial retreat uses the measured remaining
  distance on resume. If that line cannot be completed, it stops before swinging;
  inspect `stop_reason`. This local guard does not check the rest of the scene.
  A grasp point below 1.0 m goes straight up by up to 6 cm. After releasing
  a held object, the return then follows a Cartesian line above the starting
  TCP at the cleared height before finishing the joint return; this protection
  persists after a separate finger retreat until homed or a new close command.
  Ordinary home keeps the original lift/joint route. An unreachable/incomplete
  staging line stops the call; inspect `stop_reason` before retrying.
  Release what you hold and clear tall things first: this is not scene collision
  checking. Between nearby
  operations, leave the arm at a clear local staging pose and continue from
  there. Use `home` for needed clearance, an otherwise obstructed view, or the
  task's final reset, rather than after every object or contact attempt.
- A press (button, switch, key) is a motion: close the gripper, come down
  pointing straight down over the point, `move_to` a grasp point 1-2 cm
  BELOW where the fingertips would touch (they reach 1.3 cm beyond the grasp
  point) with `path="line"`, then back up.  The fingertips stop where the
  button bottoms out, so `reached` may read False there: measured on
  `swap_blocks`, every command from 1 to 2.8 cm deep stopped about 7 mm below
  the button's top, the bottom of its travel.  Nothing reports whether a press
  registered; look for the effect the task expects.  Count your presses: a
  task that asks for three fails with two or four.
  Timed tracking limits can interrupt a press. With monitoring enabled, a partial
  single-arm move returns control to Astra before subsequent code runs; inspect
  the current state and plan the retreat in the next cell if interrupted.
- In a few tasks a third arm, driven by the benchmark, takes part (an
  opponent's moves, a demonstration to watch).  It moves only while native
  actions run - your motions, or `set_gripper(arm, robo.OPEN)` on an open
  gripper, which spends about 2 actions and moves nothing - and an opponent
  may wait until both your arms are back near their start before it plays.
  When the instruction says to wait or to watch, home both arms and let time
  pass that way, looking at the head camera, until it has finished.
- For legacy geometric stepping: a 10 cm line costs about 5 native actions, a 40 cm swing
  with a 90 degree turn about 15; `steps_used` reports it.  Every native action
  counts against the episode's budget (the task card states it; `Gripper.step`
  / `Gripper.step_limit` tell you where you stand).  When it is spent the
  state reports `truncated` and no motion is possible.

## Advanced motion controls and contact capabilities
- Read `robo.motion_capabilities()`. `card.json` retains the full startup
  capability snapshot and observation provenance; the prompt shows a concise
  summary. This is initial data; the live query reports current support.
- The intent executor selects timing itself. For low-level calls, when
  `speed_control` is true, `move_to` and `move_both` automatically use timed
  joint splines. `motion=robo.motion_profile(velocity=1.0, acceleration=4.0,
  jerk=40.0)` selects maximum commanded joint derivatives in rad/s, rad/s²,
  rad/s³. These defaults are planning choices, not hardware ratings. Limits
  apply to every moving joint, not TCP metres per second or measured speed.
- `move_both(left=final_left, right=final_right, via=[{"left": pose1,
  "right": pose2}, ...], motion=profile)` plans all intermediate targets and
  the final targets before moving. Values are world TCP xyz or `robo.pose`.
  Omitted arms hold the preceding waypoint; xyz preserves its orientation.
  A single call uses a C2 joint spline (continuous position, velocity and
  acceleration), with rest at the endpoints. Intermediate points do not add
  settle pauses; a reversal can still require a joint to slow to zero.
- Smoothing removes redundant IK samples within 0.0001 rad per joint, then
  blends joint paths. Every emitted substep is checked against joint limits
  and arm/table geometry. For line moves and `move_both`, sampled deviation
  from the IK path is bounded to 3 mm / 1 degree; requested relative-pose
  checks still apply. This is not full object/inter-arm collision checking.
- Only group motions that can proceed without a new observation, contact
  decision or release. Gripper commands apply from the first native action
  throughout the route. Use separate calls for decisions at intermediate points.
- `trajectory_clock` comes from the client, never network latency. The current
  XPL adapter supplies position AND velocity targets at physical substeps and
  bypasses the native arms' per-action interpolation/hold. Gripper and support
  arm processing stay native. Continuity is in **simulation time within one
  call**; network/model waits and interruptions have no wall-clock continuity
  guarantee. Separate calls start/end at rest. `home` retains geometric stepping.
- For explicit low-level moves, `motion="native"` is available for observed clear empty-hand travel and clear
  straight transport after a broad stable grasp has been verified. Keep a
  delicate, thin, slipping or uncertain grasp on smooth `carry`. This restores
  the original waypoint executor and simulator interpolation, with no C2 or
  derivative-limit guarantee. Geometry checks, gripper KEEP, receipts and resume
  still apply. A native tracking miss holds the current target only within
  the shared progress/correction limits; otherwise it stops before advancing.
  Moving timed splines stop on a tracking miss. `path="joint"` can save steps where the whole swing is clear;
  use `path="line"` where orientation/path shape matters. Never choose a joint
  swing for a fragile grasp merely because its preview is cheaper.
- Choose `motion="transit"` for smooth empty-hand travel, `"approach"` for the
  final approach, `"carry"` for delicate held-object transport, and `"precision"` for
  placement/insertion. Omitted motion keeps 1/4/40 limits; explicit carry uses
  1.8/10/120. Approach uses 1/6/90, precision 0.45/3/45. All phases ramp smoothly
  from/to rest. Phase choice depends on task intent, observed clearance and
  object stability, not gripper opening alone. Transit is not collision checking.
  Move quickly to an observed pre-grasp region, slow before contact, verify the
  grasp, then carry smoothly with `path="line"` and unchanged orientation for
  fragile or thin pinches. Prefer a broader grasp region when available; a short
  lift alone does not establish stability during rotation or handoff.
  Do not use precision speed for the whole journey.
  Split where new observations, grasp/release or contact decisions are needed;
  use via only for a route that can proceed without those decisions.
- Use `robo.motion_window(far="transit", near="approach", distance_m=0.02)`
  for a long descent through observed free space: only the final path region
  uses slow limits, with no stop at the speed transition. For placement choose
  far="carry", near="precision"; for departure use at="start". Choose the
  window from geometry; distance is nominal path travel, not sensed clearance.
  Crossing IK segments use near limits in full. Pure rotations remain slow.
  Dual-arm windows share a path metric, not independent arm contact distances.
  Verify a grasp with a short lift (often 1–2 cm if visible and clear), then
  carry; do not spend a long lift merely discovering a failed grasp. After
  release, clear the contact region before faster empty-hand travel.
- Preview costly travel and allocate the whole remaining operation before
  grasping, including handoff, placement/insertion and return. Preposition the
  receiving arm when useful and choose a handoff region reachable by both arms.
  Choose a stable donor grasp first, even if exposing the receiving grasp then
  needs reorientation. Approach exposed material beyond the donor fingers, not
  the donor TCP; account for finger width and opening. Narrow the receiving
  opening when useful, check a wrist view, and use approach/precision for the
  final 1–2 cm. A reachable TCP pair does not prove the grippers fit together.
  Each preview starts from CURRENT state, so separate previews do not simulate
  a future sequence. Use via to join already-decided route sections, while
  preserving stops where a new observation or grasp/release decision is needed.
- `move_to(..., motion="transit", preview=True)` and
  `move_both(..., preview=True)` return a planning dict without moving arms or
  grippers. Check `accepted`, `trajectory.planned_steps`, `planned_duration_s`,
  `limiting_factor` and the remaining budget before costly travel. Over-budget
  rejections retain the estimate. Reserve steps for grasping, manipulation and
  cleanup; do not blindly spend most of the episode approaching the first object.
  Previews never execute later by themselves; the real call replans from live
  state. Preview is not a fresh object observation or a motion permission.
- Timing is allocated per segment with shared velocity/acceleration at joins.
  Redundant straight joint samples do not add time or stops. Difficulty in one
  section does not impose its slowdown on the entire route. Existing physical
  and native timesteps, derivative bounds and geometry checks remain in force.
- Inspect `result.trajectory` for planned duration, native steps and derivative
  bounds; `steps_used` reports consumed actions. Slower profiles can exceed the
  native budget and reject before movement. Tracking misses interrupt the path;
  inspect the actual endpoint before proceeding. No unexecuted suffix is replayed.
- Legacy hosts without a verified substep clock keep their existing geometric
  moves; explicit `motion` or a nonempty `via` request rejects without movement.
- Current adapters report `force_control=False`, `force_feedback=False`,
  `gripper_force_control=False` and `impedance_control=False`. Neither obstructed
  closure nor tracking lag measures force. No target-force, force-limit stop or
  compliant-contact API is provided. The gripper remains position-controlled.

## Composing dual-arm code
- Astra chooses arm roles, target geometry, ordering, release timing and bounded
  corrections in Python. The API performs motions and reports their outcomes;
  these notes are guidance, not a prescribed task sequence.
- `move_both(left=..., right=...)` moves both on one native clock. Each target is
  world TCP xyz (retain current orientation), a `robo.pose(xyz, quat)` with unit
  quaternion [w,x,y,z], or None (hold that arm). `robo.observe()` returns both
  poses; `obs.left.shifted(dx,dy,dz)` is a world offset from that fixed snapshot.
- Compose `move_both` freely with `move_to`, `set_gripper` and Python branches.
  Two sequential calls are not simultaneous movement. `keep_relative=True` checks
  a fixed relative TCP transform; synchronizing alone does not preserve it.
- `move_both` grippers use strings `keep/open/close`, taking effect from the first
  native step. KEEP retains the last commanded squeeze. Choose whether release
  occurs during travel or at a separately verified endpoint based on actual support.
- Inspect DualMove.reached, left/right errors, status, recoverable, steps_used and
  observation before dependent work. Expected rejections and recoverable partial
  endpoints may select a bounded local correction. With Valen enabled, a partial
  endpoint is visually checked before that code continues. Critical faults and
  review requests hand the live scene back to Astra; actions are never rolled back.
- max_steps/timeout_s limit one call. Preserve planner retry/phase allocations
  across cells; the native episode counter remains cumulative. The host checks
  sampled arm/table geometry, not full arm-arm or object collision volumes. Read
  motion_capabilities before relying on a check; unsupported requests reject.

## Gripper
- `set_gripper(arm, command)` runs a full stroke (repeats the command until
  the fingers stop, 2 to 10 native actions) without moving the arms.  After a
  close, `holding` says the fingers stopped on something (anything thicker
  than about 3 mm) and `width_m` how wide it is across the fingers
  (0.002 m + 0.109 m x opening: a 3.5 cm block reads 0.035 held across two
  faces and 0.049 across its diagonal; within 1 mm on things from 3 to 8 cm).
  `set_gripper(arm, robo.OPEN, opening=0.5)` opens only that far: something
  0.002 + 0.109 x opening metres wide then just fits (0.5: 5.7 cm), to reach
  between close neighbours (slices in a rack, tools in a tray) without
  catching them.
- The fingers are position-controlled. Opening and obstructed closure do not
  measure squeeze force; there is no force target or force estimate in this API.
  Thin parts can slip: grasp near the centre of mass, turn in small steps and
  check the object's motion.
- `holding` is not proof that the INTENDED object is held stably: start with
  a short clear lift and confirm that it moves with the arm before a large
  transfer or reorientation (wrist camera, or `locate` it at its old
  place and see it gone).  A held object is hard to `locate` because the
  gripper covers it.
- Carry with `gripper=robo.CLOSE` in every `move_to` that transports an
  object; `robo.OPEN` in a motion drops it.
- Thin and flat parts (handles, boards, a set square) are easy to miss or let
  slip: close across the thin side, and compare `width_m` with the part's
  thickness before lifting; `holding` False or a width far from it is a miss.
- A top grasp with these tools: `locate` the object; `set_gripper(arm, OPEN)`;
  `move_to` to a reachable clearance above it pointing down (`pitch=90`,
  choose height as described in Motion) and
  `locate` it again from there (see Perception); turn to `yaw=grasp_yaw` and
  descend with `path="line"` until the pads cover the top part of the object
  (a 3.5 cm block: grasp point about 1.8 cm below its top, and never lower
  than 1.6 cm above the table: a target below that is refused);
  `set_gripper(arm, CLOSE)`; lift with `path="line"`; confirm.  A side grasp
  of an upright object: level (`pitch=0, roll=0`: the fingers close
  horizontally), `yaw` pointing from the arm's base at the object, grasp
  point 3-5 cm above its top; then down with `path="line"`, the open fingers
  passing on either side of it, to about half its height.  An approach from
  short of the object, towards the robot, is often out of reach this close to
  the base.
- To put something down, lower it with `path="line"` until its bottom is just
  above the surface (support top + object height, plus a few mm), open, and
  lift straight up before moving sideways.

## Perception
- On a native/XPL snapshot backend, first take `view = robo.snapshot()`
  (latest record) or `robo.snapshot(refresh=True)` (explicit read-only capture).
  Use `robo.image(camera, step=view['step'])` and pass
  `observation_id=view['observation_id']` to `locate`, `segment`,
  `back_project`, and `camera_meta` to keep image, depth and calibration together.
  Historical snapshots may be analysed; their coordinates are not current targets.
- Perception results carry `provenance`: capture/session, image/calibration
  sources, geometry method and assumptions. First check `found`, nonempty
  coordinates, `note` and geometric plausibility. Keep the result and use
  `robo.require_current(result)` before depending on it: this checks its source,
  not identity, grasp quality or task completion. Motion, a new capture or
  unresolved execution invalidates currentness; `current_at_return` is historical.
  Initial measurements can inform planning, but do not certify later targets.
  Plain xyz tuples carry no automatic dependency; full details remain in provenance.
- These bindings require backend support. Legacy adapters retain unverified
  perception and refuse an explicit observation ID instead of silently ignoring it.
- Three cameras, 640x480.  `cam_head` is the authority for identity and coarse
  geometry: which object, which destination, the overall layout.  The wrist
  cameras show what an arm is about to grasp or is holding.
- `locate(text, camera)` segments the object and measures it in 3D from the
  depth image: `center_xyz` (footprint centre, half its height), `top_z`,
  `height_m`, the footprint `length_m` x `width_m` (the smallest rectangle
  around it), its long axis `long_axis_deg` (from +x) and `grasp_yaw`, the
  `yaw` that closes pointing-down fingers across its narrow side (a cube's:
  across two faces).  `center_xyz` is the middle of that rectangle: a handle,
  a spout or a tail belongs to the footprint and pulls the middle away from
  the body's (a piggy bank's by 1.2 cm, a mug's by about 1 cm).  It measures
  what the camera sees, and fills in the far side of an object that is round
  about an upright axis (a bottle, a cup) from the arc that is seen; what
  other things or the robot's own gripper hide is not measured, so a partly
  hidden object measures smaller and off-centre (move the arm away, or
  measure from a wrist camera).  A hollow one (bowl, bin) gives its rim as
  `top_z`.  Nothing moves.
- `segment` + `back_project` give one mask and one surface point (pass the
  mask's `centroid` with the SAME camera); use them for a pixel that `locate`
  does not cover, e.g. a spot on the table.
- By default snapshot-capable calls read the latest recorded observation;
  request a new capture explicitly when needed. Localise, act, then localise
  again. Positions from before a contact are stale.
- A mask is the best match for the text, not proof of identity.  Describe one
  object by colour, shape and position; check `score` and where `centroid`
  falls in the image before acting on it.  If a name finds nothing, try what
  it looks like (`"yellow cube"` for a textured block).  Words like "left"
  or "front" do not make it choose another of several alike objects: when two
  prompts give the same centre they found the same object; tell instances
  apart in the image (or with `segment`'s box) instead.  Mask pixels that lie
  on the robot's own arm are dropped; a mask entirely on it returns a `note`
  saying so.
- `robo.image(camera)` returns the recorded picture as an array for your own
  analysis.

## Judging progress
- Nothing reports task success while the episode runs: no result carries a
  task-success flag.  Judge progress from the images and the robot state,
  and say so when you cannot tell.
- Grasp: the target leaves its place and moves with the arm.
- An amount the instruction names ("lift it by 10 cm") is measured on the
  object, not on the gripper: a held object sags and slips a little in the
  fingers.  Go 2-3 cm beyond the amount asked.
- Transport: the hold stays stable through the lift and the lateral move.
- Placement: the object rests where the instruction wants it after the
  gripper has opened and the arm has withdrawn; an object on a rim or beside
  its destination is not placed.
- The benchmark checks the final scene strictly: positions to about a
  centimetre and angles to a few degrees of the arrangement asked (a row is
  straight, a stack centred, a part upright); EVERY object the instruction
  names; containers and fixtures where they were and upright; the exact
  number of presses; in a task that asks to push or slide something, lifting
  it can fail the task.  "Nearly" is a failure.  Before finishing, `locate`
  each object again, compare with what is asked, and correct what is off
  while budget remains.
- What else is checked: objects you did not mean to move (things you knock
  over or out of place count against you: reach between close neighbours
  with a narrower opening); plurals ("the wooden blocks" means all of them,
  whatever their colour); articulated parts pushed to the end of their travel
  (a lever most of the way down, a lid shut within a few degrees; a free lid
  swings when touched); liquids: nearly all of it in the target at the moment
  the container comes back upright, and the container upright at the end -
  liquid visible in a cup is not enough.
- Most tasks count as done only once BOTH arms are back at the pose they
  started in (within 0.15 m and 20 degrees) with BOTH grippers open (an
  opening above 0.8, for a few tasks 0.9), the scene being as the instruction
  wants it at that moment - whether or not the instruction says to reset the
  arms. Account for applicable cleanup in the task acceptance conditions. Complete
  the required manipulation before cleanup; do not reserve a guessed return
  allowance or abandon useful task progress because of a cost forecast. First
  establish where held objects can be supported; choose release and retreat
  order from the scene. Homing/releasing is not harmless in every configuration.
- Finish when the required task state and applicable cleanup conditions hold.

## Safety and recovery
- Inspect the scene after motion.  Stop the current plan if an object is
  unstable or an arm is obstructed, and re-localise before recovering.
- An object that has left the table may be unrecoverable.
- After a failed phase name what went wrong (wrong object, missed grasp, lost
  hold, unreachable target, premature release) and change one meaningful
  variable; do not repeat the same target or the same recovery twice.
- There is exactly one episode and no reset.  Recover in place: re-observe,
  re-localise, re-grasp.
